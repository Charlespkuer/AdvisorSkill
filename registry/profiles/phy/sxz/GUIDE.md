# Physics writing guide for phy/sxz

These suggestions were drawn from one manuscript review. Consider the strength of the evidence and the target venue when applying them.

## Make claims easy to interpret

- Name the subject and the quantities involved in each claim. For a comparison, identify the baseline and the quantity being compared.
- State what was observed and the conditions under which it holds. Use direct negation when it conveys an exclusion, falsification, or negative-control result.
- Prefer concrete descriptions of physical behavior. Define abstract labels when readers need them to interpret a claim.

## Open with the physical question

In the abstract and introduction, establish the physical setting, why it matters, and the research question before introducing procedures or fitting choices.
